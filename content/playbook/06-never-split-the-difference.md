# Never Split the Difference — Chris Voss
## Distilled playbook (frameworks in own words, applied to agri-finance origination)

> Role in the system: the NEGOTIATION layer. Added after research review — the original five
> books cover trust, conversation method, psychology, fundamentals, and network, but none of
> them covers late-stage tension: the rate conversation, the incumbent bank's retention
> counter-offer, the family member who digs in. Voss's FBI-derived toolkit is built for
> emotionally charged, high-stakes negotiation — which is exactly what agri refinance and
> succession conversations become at the pointy end. It complements rather than contradicts
> the trust frame: tactical empathy IS low self-orientation under pressure.

---

## 1. Tactical empathy — the master skill

Understand the other side's feelings and worldview, then demonstrate that understanding out
loud. Not agreement — recognition. People soften when they feel genuinely understood.
*Agri: "Twenty-two years with one bank — walking away from that feels like a betrayal of the
relationship, even when the numbers say move."* Said FOR the client before they say it.

Maps directly onto Maister's Intimacy and acknowledge-before-answer — this is the same move
executed under negotiation pressure.

## 2. Mirroring

Repeat the last one to three critical words of what they just said, with an upward inflection,
then go silent. Triggers elaboration without a single question.
- Client: "The bank says they'll match any offer anyway."
- You: "Match any offer?" *(silence — they explain what was actually offered, and the holes in it.)*
*The cheapest discovery tool in existence. Drill: produce the mirror, then hold the silence.*

## 3. Labeling

Name the emotion or position tentatively: "It seems like…", "It sounds like…", "It feels like
you're worried that…". A correct label defuses negatives and reinforces positives; an
incorrect one costs nothing because the framing invites correction ("It seems like the rate is
what matters" — "No, it's not the rate, it's that nobody answers the phone").
*Never follow a label with "but". Label, then silence.*

## 4. The accusation audit

Open by listing the worst things the other side could think or say about you — before they do.
*Agri, first referrer meeting: "You're probably thinking — another city banker who'll be in a
different job in eighteen months, who'll churn my clients for commission and embarrass me."*
Takes the sting out of every objection on the list; what remains is conversation.
This is objection PREVENTION (Rackham ch.6) executed at the emotional level.

## 5. "No" is the start, not the end

People feel safe and in control when they say no. Don't chase "yes" (which is often counterfeit
— said to get rid of you). Ask no-oriented questions: "Is it a ridiculous idea to look at the
guarantee structure before the season starts?" "Have you given up on getting the succession
sorted before the lease comes up?"
*A grazier's "no" protects his autonomy. Give him chances to say it.*

## 6. "That's right" vs "you're right"

The breakthrough moment is when they say **"that's right"** — your summary captured their
world so completely they endorse it. "You're right" is how Australians end a conversation they
want out of. Drill the distinction: trigger "that's right" with a summary (paraphrase +
labelled emotions), never settle for "you're right".

## 7. Calibrated questions — "How" and "What"

Open questions starting with How or What that make the other side solve your problem and give
them the illusion of control. The flagship: **"How am I supposed to do that?"** — a no that
keeps the conversation alive.
- *Bank retention offer on the table: "What makes this offer compelling to you now when it
  wasn't available for the last three years?"*
- *Referrer demanding exclusivity: "How can I protect your name with your clients if I can't
  see the whole file?"*
- *Internal: credit committee pushback — "What would need to be true for this to clear appetite?"*
Calibrated questions are SPIN's implication/need-payoff engine pointed at negotiation.

## 8. Anchors, ranges, and the rate conversation

- Let them anchor first when information is on their side; when you must go first, anchor with
  a RANGE referencing market data ("facilities like this are printing between X and Y").
- Beware the incumbent's retention anchor: a sudden rate match is priced to the cost of
  switching, not the value of the relationship. Label it: "It sounds like they've sharpened the
  pencil now that you're walking."
- Never split the difference on things that matter: a half-fixed half-variable compromise that
  suits nobody is worse than a structure argued from the client's cash-flow reality.
- Non-round numbers ($4.87m, 6.13%) signal calculation, not haggling.

## 9. Black swans — the unknown unknowns

Every negotiation has 3+ pieces of hidden information that change everything. They surface in
unguarded moments — the kitchen table before the meeting, the walk to the ute after it. The
brother-in-law on the guarantee. The neighbour's block coming up. The diagnosis nobody mentions.
*App hook: the debrief coach should explicitly ask "what did you learn in the unguarded
moments?" and write it to the contact record.*

## 10. The 7-38-55 rule and voice

Content is a fraction of the message; tone and delivery carry the rest. Default voice:
the **late-night FM DJ voice** — calm, slow, downward-inflected — for tense moments.
Positive/playful voice for everything else. Never the assertive voice on a farming family.
*This is why the app trains by VOICE: tone is a trainable skill text can't carry.*
