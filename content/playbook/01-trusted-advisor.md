# The Trusted Advisor — Maister, Green & Galford
## Distilled playbook (frameworks in own words, applied to agri-finance origination)

> Role in the system: This is Tom's OPERATING MODEL. Every other book plugs into this.
> In agri broking/origination, the referrer relationship (accountants, turnaround specialists,
> advisors) and the farming-family relationship ARE the product. Priority: HIGHEST.

---

## 1. The Trust Equation (the central model)

**T = (C + R + I) / S**

- **C — Credibility**: Do they believe what you say? Words, credentials, demonstrated expertise.
  *Agri: knowing water licence lending policy, livestock seasonality, succession structures cold.*
- **R — Reliability**: Do they believe you'll do what you say? Actions, consistency over time.
  *Agri: every "I'll come back to you Friday" honoured. Referrers refer on reliability.*
- **I — Intimacy**: Do they feel safe sharing with you? Emotional closeness, discretion.
  *Agri: succession is family conflict, debt stress, pride. The banker who can sit at the kitchen
  table and hear the hard stuff wins. Most undervalued lever in finance.*
- **S — Self-orientation** (the DIVISOR): How much do you appear to be in it for yourself?
  High S destroys everything above the line. One self-oriented act can halve perceived trust.
  *Agri: pushing your product when refinance isn't right; talking commission; rushing to pitch.*

**Key insight**: Most finance professionals over-invest in C (technical mastery) and
under-invest in I and lowering S — which is where differentiation actually lives.

### Self-orientation tells (drill these as recognition items)
- Relating their story back to yourself
- Premature problem-solving (jumping to the solution to look smart)
- Wanting to be seen as right rather than be useful
- Listening to respond, not to understand
- Fear-driven behaviours: fear of looking ignorant, of losing the deal, of silence

## 2. The Trust Development Process (5 stages — the conversation arc)

1. **Engage** — Earn the right to the conversation. A credible, client-relevant opening that
   shows you've done homework. *"I've been watching what the basis swing did to your forward
   contracts this season"* — not *"let me tell you about Macquarie's offering."*
2. **Listen** — Earn the right to comment. Active, acknowledging listening. The client must
   FEEL heard before they'll hear you. (Maps to SPIN's investigating stage.)
3. **Frame** — Crystallise the real issue, cleanly and without blame. Often the most valuable
   single act: naming the problem the family hasn't been able to say out loud.
   *"So the real issue isn't the rate — it's that the facility structure doesn't match your
   cash-flow cycle, and your brother-in-law's name is on the guarantee."*
4. **Envision** — Jointly imagine the end state before discussing solutions. What does good
   look like in 3 years? Who's on the title? What's the debt structure?
5. **Commit** — Agree concrete next steps with mutual obligations. (Maps to SPIN "advance".)

**Failure mode to drill**: skipping from Engage straight to solutions ("premature solving").
The sequence is the discipline.

---

## 3. The Relationship Depth Ladder

1. **Service-offering based** — you're a vendor; conversations are about the product/transaction
2. **Needs based** — you solve the stated problem
3. **Relationship based** — you provide insight beyond the brief; they call you early
4. **Trust based** — you advise on whatever matters; "safe haven for hard issues"; you're in
   the room before the deal exists

*Agri application: a referrer at level 1 sends you scraps. A referrer at level 4 brings you
into the succession conversation two years before the refinance. The CRM should tag every
relationship with its current rung and a "next rung" action.*

---

## 4. Traits of trusted advisors (selected, drillable)

- Focus on the client, not their own ego/firm/products
- Believe relationship > current transaction (long game)
- Are reliably consistent in small things (small commitments kept = trust compounding)
- Tell hard truths early and kindly (condition-of-trust, not threat to it)
- Treat client emotion as data, not as an obstacle
- "Help first, get hired second" — generosity of insight before engagement

---

## 5. Practical trust-builders (behavioural drill set)

- Give value away in the first meeting (insight, benchmark, intro) — no strings
- Take notes on the personal, not just financial (kids' names, the dog, the back paddock plan)
- Use the client's language, not bank language ("the home block", not "primary security")
- Say "I don't know, I'll find out" fast and cleanly — credibility paradox: admitting limits raises C
- Return calls inside the day even with "no update yet" — reliability signalling
- Never bad-mouth the incumbent bank — raises your S, lowers your I
- Acknowledge before answering: name the emotion/stake first, then respond to content

---

## 6. Romance vs. transaction framing

Clients decide with emotion and justify with logic. The technical answer (rate, LVR, structure)
is the entry ticket, not the differentiator. The differentiator is how it FEELS to deal with you:
safe, understood, unhurried, on their side.

*Origination KPI implication: optimise for "would they ring me first?" not "did I win this deal?"*
