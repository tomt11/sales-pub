# The SPIN Selling Fieldbook — Neil Rackham
## Distilled playbook (frameworks in own words, applied to agri-finance origination)

> Role in the system: the CONVERSATION METHODOLOGY. How Tom runs client and referrer
> meetings inside the trust frame. Built from Huthwaite's research on 35,000+ sales calls:
> in large/complex sales, successful sellers ASK more and pitch later. Agri lending is a
> textbook large sale: high value, long cycle, multiple stakeholders (family), high risk of a
> bad decision for the buyer.

---

## 1. The four stages of a sales call

1. **Preliminaries** — brief; establish who you are and why you're there. Don't oversell here.
2. **Investigating** — the core. Uncover and DEVELOP needs via SPIN questions. Where deals
   are won.
3. **Demonstrating capability** — show how you can help, expressed as BENEFITS to explicit needs.
4. **Obtaining commitment** — close on an ADVANCE, not a vague continuation.

---

## 2. SPIN question types (the heart of the method)

### S — Situation questions
Facts and background. *"How many entities hold the land?" "What's the current facility mix?"*
- Necessary but LOW VALUE to the client — every banker asks these. Successful sellers ask FEW,
  because they did homework. **Drill: convert situation questions into research done beforehand.**

### P — Problem questions
Probe difficulties, dissatisfactions. *"How's the working capital limit holding up through
harvest?" "Any frustrations with how the bank handled the drought year?"*
- Surface IMPLIED needs. More strongly linked to success than situation questions.

### I — Implication questions (the differentiator)
Take a stated problem and grow its perceived size by exploring consequences.
*"If the limit caps out mid-harvest again, what does that do to your ability to sell at the
price you want rather than when cash forces you?" "How does the uncertainty affect the
succession discussion with your daughter?"*
- The hardest to ask, most strongly correlated with success in large sales.
- Builds the pain BEFORE the solution, so the solution lands with weight.
- **This is Tom's #1 skill gap to train** (true for nearly all technically-strong bankers,
  who jump from problem straight to solution).

### N — Need-payoff questions
Get the CLIENT to articulate the value of solving it. *"If the facility tracked your commodity
cycle, what would that be worth come November?" "How would it help the family conversation if
the guarantee structure were cleaned up?"*
- The client persuades themselves. Reduces objections instead of creating them.
- Also rehearses the internal champion to sell on your behalf — critical for referrers:
  a Need-payoff conversation with an accountant equips them to pitch YOU to their client.

**Sequence discipline: P → I builds the problem up; N builds the solution's value up.
Never pitch before the need is explicit.**

---

## 3. Implied vs explicit needs

- **Implied need**: a statement of problem/dissatisfaction ("the bank's been slow lately")
- **Explicit need**: a statement of want ("we need a lender who can move inside 30 days")
- In large sales, implied needs do NOT predict buying. Explicit needs do.
- The seller's job: convert implied → explicit using I and N questions.
- **Equation of value**: client buys when *seriousness of problem > cost of solving it*.
  Implication questions raise the left side.

---

## 4. Features / Advantages / Benefits (FAB)

- **Feature**: fact about the offering ("Macquarie offers 12-month interest-only periods")
- **Advantage**: how it generically helps ("which smooths cash flow")
- **Benefit**: how it meets an EXPLICIT need THIS client has stated
  ("which solves the harvest squeeze you said costs you your November selling position")
- Research finding: benefits (in this strict sense) drive success; features late in the call
  slightly HURT; advantages plateau. **Drill: classify statements as F/A/B; rewrite F→B.**

---

## 5. Obtaining commitment: Advances vs Continuations

- **Advance**: a specific action moving the sale forward — by them or you.
  *"You'll send the last two years' financials by Friday"; "We'll meet with your accountant
  Tuesday"; "I'll table indicative terms at the family meeting on the 20th."*
- **Continuation**: warm words, no action. *"Great chat, let's keep in touch."* Feels good,
  predicts nothing.
- Plan EVERY interaction (including a referrer coffee) with: best-case advance, minimum
  acceptable advance. **The field-application loop should ask for these in every pre-brief.**

---

## 6. Objections: prevention over handling

- Most objections are SELLER-CREATED: pitching solutions before value is built (too many
  features → price objections; advantages too early → "we don't need that").
- Cure: more I and N questions earlier, fewer features. If objections recur in roleplay,
  the grader should flag the earlier sequencing error, not just the response quality.
- Genuine objections: acknowledge → clarify with a question → respond → confirm.

---

## 7. Planning a call (the Fieldbook's core discipline)

Before each meeting write down:
1. Call objective as an ADVANCE (specific, actionable)
2. Likely problems THIS client has that you can solve (pick 2–3, don't boil the ocean)
3. For each: prepared P, I, N questions (write actual sentences, not topics)
4. Which explicit needs you hope to surface; which capabilities map to them as BENEFITS

**This planning sheet IS the app's meeting pre-brief template.**
