# How to Win Friends and Influence People — Dale Carnegie
## Distilled playbook (principles in own words, applied to agri-finance origination)

> Role in the system: the INTERPERSONAL FUNDAMENTALS. Oldest, simplest, and the layer most
> visible to clients in the first 90 seconds. Many principles overlap Maister (intimacy,
> low self-orientation) and Cialdini (liking) — the curriculum teaches it AFTER those so the
> overlaps reinforce rather than repeat.

---

## Part 1 — Fundamental techniques in handling people
1. **Don't criticise, condemn or complain.** Criticism triggers defence, not change.
   *Agri: never criticise the incumbent bank, the previous broker, or — fatally — a decision
   the patriarch made in 1998. Critique structures, never people.*
2. **Give honest and sincere appreciation.** The deepest human craving is to feel important.
   Appreciation ≠ flattery: it must be specific and true.
   *Agri: "the way you've held this place through two droughts is seriously impressive" —
   when you mean it.*
3. **Arouse in the other person an eager want.** Frame everything in THEIR interests.
   Pre-Rackham version of need-payoff: talk about what they want and show how to get it.

## Part 2 — Six ways to make people like you
1. Become genuinely interested in other people (genuine — the operative word)
2. Smile
3. **A person's name is the sweetest sound** — use names; remember the kids', the dog's,
   the property's name. *App hook: CRM should store and surface these before meetings.*
4. **Be a good listener; encourage others to talk about themselves** — the best
   conversationalist is the best listener
5. Talk in terms of the other person's interests (do the homework on what they care about)
6. Make the other person feel important — sincerely

## Part 3 — Twelve ways to win people to your thinking (selected, highest-value)
1. **The only way to win an argument is to avoid it** — winning the argument loses the deal
2. Never say "you're wrong" — show respect for their opinion
3. **If you're wrong, admit it quickly and emphatically** — disarms completely; raises trust
4. Begin in a friendly way
5. **Get a "yes, yes" early** — start where you agree (consistency principle, Cialdini ch.7)
6. Let the other person do most of the talking (SPIN convergence: investigating > pitching)
7. **Let them feel the idea is theirs** — the strongest move in family succession work:
   the structure "the family came up with" gets implemented; yours gets resisted
8. Try honestly to see things from their point of view
9. **Be sympathetic to their ideas and desires** — "I don't blame you one bit for feeling
   that way; in your position I'd feel the same" (acknowledge-before-answer, Maister)
10. Appeal to nobler motives (legacy, the kids, the land — agri's nobler motives are vivid)
11. Dramatise your ideas — show, don't just tell (charts, the actual cash-flow picture)
12. Throw down a challenge (use sparingly; works on competitive operators)

## Part 4 — Be a leader: changing people without offence (for managing deals/teams)
- Begin with praise and honest appreciation; call attention to errors indirectly
- Talk about your own mistakes before criticising
- **Ask questions instead of giving direct orders** (converges with SPIN entirely)
- Let the other person save face — paramount in family-business dynamics
- Praise the slightest improvement; give a fine reputation to live up to
- Make the fault seem easy to correct; make them happy about doing what you suggest
